## The flow

1. **Brief** (desk) — club, season, length (0.5–25 min), visibility, optional scheduled time (Athens). Optional (3.11.2026):
   a **focus** window (one tie, both legs — `research.focus`, totals recounted from what is left), **quotes and
   headlines** one per line with their https source (`quote | speaker | role | words | url`, `press | outlet | date |
   headline | url` → `research.note_claims`), and **hold for my pictures** (the job is created paused; the owner uploads
   pictures into `jobs/<id>/art-<name>` through `/api/master/stories/<id>/art`, then presses Resume). Saved as a job in
   the durable store (`stories/jobs/<id>.json`, compare-and-swap).
2. **Worker** (`.github/workflows/stories-worker.yml`, every 10 min) asks `/api/master/stories/worker` (`CRON_SECRET`):
   `peek` → `claim` (6.5 h lease) → `art_urls` (the owner's pictures, ingested as `beat:job-<id>/…`) → `progress`
   (pause/cancel answered on every report) → `upload_urls` → `finish`. The Shorts the script proposes are cut from the
   master (`shorts.auto`, ≤58 s, 9:16) and uploaded beside it; the desk plays them.
3. **Pipeline** (`scripts/football-stories/studio/`, Python):
   research ledger (archive + dossier claims, every row with a source) → script (Claude / OpenAI / Gemini, validated:
   every number in narration and on screen must come from a cited claim) → voice (ElevenLabs if the key exists, else
   free Kokoro `bm_george`) → graphics (Playwright cards in The Weekly's type) → edit (paper collage, camera moves,
   owner cut-outs, owner beat illustrations) → audio (synthesised score, owner crowd recordings, −14 LUFS) → master,
   preview, captions, chapters, description with sources, 3 thumbnails → QA (streams, sync, loudness, black, captions,
   rival colours, contact sheet).
4. **Review** (desk) — the script, the preview, thumbnails, checks, the YouTube package. Approve, retry, cancel.
5. **Upload** — through **YouTube Studio**, never the API (API uploads from an unaudited project are locked private). The
   desk records only what Studio shows: started → done (video id + visibility) or uncertain. The **publish kit** lists
   every Studio step in order (playlist, audience, altered-content answer, captions, end screen, card, Shorts) with copy
   buttons for the title, description and tags.

## Other clubs (2.11.2026)

Every club but Hapoel Tel Aviv reads its compiled FAN LIFE pack (`content/compiled/<club>.json`, built by
`npm run clubs:precompile`): approved rows with an https source and confidence 2, the season taken from the date
(July–June). Competitions carry the name they had THEN — UEFA files every UEFA Cup tie as the Europa League, the film
says UEFA Cup before 2009/10. Scorers print only when the record names every goal. A second leg knows whether the club
went through (aggregate, then away goals) and stamps THROUGH! or OUT instead of WIN/LOST. Map pins come from
`dossiers/places.json`; a domestic map highlights the club's own country. Film 2: Celtic 2002/03, `dossiers/celtic-2002-03*`.

## Beat art (owner illustrations)

`studio/kit/beats/<club id>-<season>/` — colour paper-cut pictures made for one story, with `index.json` (key,
description, source file, sha). A script names them as `"art"` or `"backdrop": "beat:<set>/<slug>"`; a key from another
club's set is refused. Colours are kept; any pixel in the club's rival family is scrubbed at render time (`paper.scrub`),
and behind a card the picture is darkened. An owner picture switches off the automatic cut-out props on that shot.
The writer is shown the set's descriptions, so a new film of the same season picks from them by itself.

Hapoel Tel Aviv 1999/00: 30 pieces, supplied 2.11.2026 (AI-generated by the owner, fictional figures from behind).

**Common set** (`beat:common/…`, `clip:common/…`) serves every club. Each piece lists the colour families it prints;
the writer is not offered a piece whose family the club's rival owns. A piece marked `"accent": "key-red"` is drawn with
one signal red (#C8102E) and the studio repaints that red into the club's own colour (`paper.recolor`).

**Cut-outs** (`studio/kit/cutouts/common/*.webp`, `cut:common/…`): figures, crowd rows and close-ups with alpha,
cut from the owner's white or paper backgrounds by `cutout.py`. Each carries a `motion` (run · dive · jump · rise · pop ·
row). A script may name them as `"figure"` on any visual; otherwise the editor chooses by the beat — a win brings a
celebrating player, a defeat a kneeling one, a shoot-out a diving keeper, a matchday a crowd row along the bottom.
People stand BEHIND the card, crowd rows rise in FRONT. Variants (`-b-`, `-c-`) are picked by club and shot, so films of
different clubs do not repeat a drawing; key red is repainted into the club's colour.

**Captions** are burned in word by word (`captions.py`); the YouTube caption track is delivered as well. **Every film ends
on the site** (`script.with_cta`): the narrator's closing line and the end card with the badge, six feature tiles and the
address.

**Clips** (`studio/kit/clips/<set>/*.mp4`, silent h.264, ~10 s): a moving world. Decoded in order at 25 fps, scrubbed and
recoloured per frame, played once and held on the last frame.

**The owner's Drive library** (609 files, 3.11.2026) is listed in `docs/fanlife/drive-library/` (README + inventory.json).
Before asking for new art, look there first; bring a folder in with Actions → *Drive art into the studio*
(`studio.drive_sync`, opens a pull request).

New batches: `python3 scripts/football-stories/ingest-art.py <zip> [--set <club>-<season>]` (names are the keys in
`docs/fanlife/55-football-stories-art-prompts.md`).

## Rules

- No fake progress, uploads or success: every state is reported by the worker or by YouTube Studio.
- Every number is sourced (`fact_guard`); a scorer is printed only when the archive names every goal.
- The paper-cut kit draws no real person's face. **Owner pictures drawn to look like a photograph or a document** (owner,
  3.11.2026: "these are drawings based on a source, they may be used") are allowed: `illustrated: true` prints
  ILLUSTRATION on screen, the credits say so, and the upload answers YES to YouTube's altered-content question. Personal
  details (a name or ID on a ticket) and a real newspaper's masthead are blurred before use (`kit_ingest` masks).
- A quote is printed word for word from a claim of kind `quote` (≤40 words, the speaker as recorded); a headline only as
  the outlet printed it, set in our own type, never a copied masthead. Neither is ever paraphrased onto the screen.
- Every film asks once to follow the channel (CLAUDE.md rule 136, art in doc 58).
- Crowd sound (owner, 3.11.2026): no continuous crowd bed; only a short, distant cheer (≤2.6 s, 8 s apart) at a goal or
  the final whistle, filtered and ducked under the narrator. Per film: soft (default) or none.
- Rival colours (rule 95) are checked on sampled frames of every film.

## Sound and look (3.11.2026)

- **Music is the owner's choice of track** (`kit/music/index.json`, `--music track:<id>`, brief `music`): the editor
  (`audio.track_score`) lays the quiet passages under tension and the full band under a goal, carried on from where
  it stopped, crossfaded, ducked under the narrator. The synthesised score stays only as the fallback. Generated
  "Balkan brass" was tried and rejected by the owner (3.11.2026) — never generate the music again.
- **Delivery** per beat (`delivery`: hush · tense · normal · punch · excited, or from the beat's mood): pace, the
  silence between sentences, a held breath at "…" or a spaced dash, and level.
- **Look** at the master (`--look`): `archive` (warm, dense, moving grain, soft vignette — the documentary),
  `punch`, `clean`.
- **The owner's own footage** (photos, phone clips from the stand) can carry a documentary: clips are tone-mapped from
  HDR and set on their own blurred frame, sound and metadata removed; the asset ledger lists them as real footage.
  Owner footage is never committed (it lives on Drive): pull it with the encrypted `drive-pull` route. The same holds
  for his photos (index rows flagged `owner_photo` stay; the files are gitignored) and for a licensed track he supplies
  (Pixabay: not redistributable on its own).
- **The music library** (owner, 3.11.2026): Tanner Helland's free-music (CC BY 4.0) — 42 original tracks in
  `kit/music/index.json` with a `url`, fetched on first use into `kit/music/cache/` (gitignored). Every film that uses
  one prints "Music by Tanner Helland" with the link in its description. His covers and remixes of other people's
  works are left out. A track's mood is "listen first" until someone has listened.
- **The publish file is one encode at the bitrate the cap allows** (`matchday/film.py`): phone footage with grain was
  450 MB at CRF 19, and a CRF ladder meant five slow encodes.

## The narrator (4.11.2026)

Owner: *"a narrator whose English is human, who says every club, player and competition right, as human and real as possible."*

1. **Names come from a lexicon, not from a guess.** `studio/kit/pronounce/lexicon.json` holds every name the films say,
   in standard IPA. Rule: a person or a club is said the way its own people say it, in English sounds (AEK = AH-ek,
   Olympiacos stressed on the last syllable, Mourinho mo-REEN-yoo); a world star keeps the form the football world uses
   (Rivaldo, Roberto Carlos, Ajax); a city with an English name keeps it (Athens, Piraeus, Seville). The longer key wins,
   so José Mourinho (Portuguese) and José Luis Mendilibar (Spanish) differ. A film can add its own in
   `dossiers/<club>-pronounce.json` — IPA (`/…/`) or a respelling.
2. **The rest of the English goes through misaki**, the G2P the voice was trained on (British lexicon, stress, the
   voice's own diphthong symbols), instead of raw espeak. Without misaki installed the old espeak path still works, with
   the names spliced in. `studio/pronounce.py` is shared with I WAS THERE (`matchday/render.py`), where an episode's own
   `ipa` table still wins.
3. **A narrator's timing:** the gap after a sentence follows the sentence (longer after a question or a short hard line,
   never the same twice), each line drifts a little in pace, a soft breath before a long line (`STORIES_BREATHS=0`
   removes it). Seeded by the text, so a beat always sounds the same.
4. **Spoken numbers:** 3–3 = "three all", 0–0 = "nil nil", 2–0 = "two nil", 1971–2026 = "1971 to 2026", 19:45 =
   "nineteen forty-five".
5. **The narrator's chain** (`studio/audio.py → NARRATOR_FX`, films and I WAS THERE): rumble and mud cut, chest and
   presence, de-esser, a little air from harmonics (the model has nothing above 12 kHz), a few ms of booth, a gentle
   leveller. `STORIES_NARRATOR_FX=0` is the A/B.
6. **What the lexicon does not know is reported:** each voiced beat lists `names` it said from the lexicon and
   `unknown` capitalised words it guessed. `tests/test_pronounce.py` fails on any accented name in a script with no entry,
   and on any entry that uses a symbol the voice cannot say.
7. **Audition:** `studio/narrator_audition.py` (workflow *Football Stories · narrator audition*) reads seven name-heavy
   lines with each candidate — the old voice, Kokoro George and Lewis after this pass, and Qwen3-TTS (Apache-2.0) with a
   British narrator designed from a description and cloned for every line — has Whisper check which names it heard,
   and sends the takes to the owner's Telegram. The takes sit on a temporary branch only while Telegram fetches them.
8. **First audition result (4.11.2026):** Whisper heard 34/41 names in Kokoro George after the pass, 33/41 before, 24/41 in
   Qwen3-TTS (respelled names read as letters: "Z-DAN", "Ray AHL"). Qwen renders at ×9.7 real time on the runner
   (a 9-minute film ≈ 90 minutes), Kokoro at ×0.5. From the transcripts: a voiced r before a consonant made "Marko"
   sound like "Marikor", so the lexicon is now spoken non-rhotic, as a British narrator speaks (Mendilibar =
   men-dee-lee-BAH); AEK said as one word was heard as "Ayak" — the owner decides between AH-ek and A-E-K.
9. **Owner's choice (4.11.2026): Take B** — Kokoro bm_george with the lexicon, misaki, human timing, breaths and the
   narrator chain is the Football Stories narrator (the default). AEK stays one word, as the Greeks say it ("כמו היוונים").
   I WAS THERE keeps its own voice (bm_lewis, series.json) and gets the same lexicon and chain.
