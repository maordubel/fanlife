# CLASSIFIED · the writers' room

Owner, 8.11.2026: *"improve, deepen, give every CLASSIFIED writer all the details and all the atmosphere — make sure
everything can go to work, with a story, a background, a gripping, viral, interesting, fun and enriching script."*

This is the brief every CLASSIFIED script is written from — by a person or by a model. FILE 01 · THE BOTTLE is the
reference: read its script before writing a new one.

## 1 · What the series is

**CLASSIFIED opens the strange files of football history.** Each FILE is one true story that sounds made up — a
phone call that put a fake striker in the Premier League, a sheikh who walked onto a World Cup pitch, a bottle of
water at Italia 90 — told as a case file on a cork board: the evidence lands, the red string runs from print to print,
the stamp comes down once a chapter.

The viewer should leave with three things: **the story** (they can tell it in the pub tonight), **the world it
happened in** (the year, the country, the football of the time — they learned something), and **the twist** (the
detail that makes them send it to a friend).

| Tab | Colour | What it is | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LAUGH** | cyan | A comedy of errors that really happened. The absurd told straight. | Deadpan, warm, never mocking the people |
| **WAR** | red | Power, crime, politics, danger — football where it should not be. | Low, taut, unhurried; let the silences work |
| **HEART** | navy | Loss, loyalty, the price somebody paid. | Close, quiet, respectful; no melodrama |

## 2 · The shape of a FILE (5–7 minutes · 650–950 narrated words)

| Movement | Time | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **COLD OPEN** | 0:00–0:40 | The object or the moment, in the present tense, one breath before the strange thing happens. Date and place in the first ten seconds. A hook law (§3). Ends on the stamp: CLASSIFIED · FILE nn · TITLE. |
| **CHAPTER 1 · THE WORLD** | ~1 min | Who, where, when — and what is at stake. Three sensory details of the era (§4). The explainer beat if the story needs a rule or a context (golden goal, the away goals rule, a 1980s transfer). |
| **CHAPTER 2 · THE SETUP** | ~1 min | The chain of small decisions that made it possible. Every link sourced. (The follow line lands after this chapter by itself — rule 136 — do not write it.) |
| **CHAPTER 3 · THE MOMENT** | ~1½ min | Slow down. One action per beat; the minute, the weather, the sound. The shout word (SMASH!, SAVED!, GONE!) lands on its word. |
| **CHAPTER 4 · THE FALLOUT** | ~1 min | What happened next: the official record, the punishments, the denials. Where the sources disagree: **VERSION A** and **VERSION B**, both told, both named. |
| **CLOSE** | 0:20 | Back to the opening object or place, changed by what we now know. One line of meaning — not a moral. "File closed." |

A pattern interrupt every 30–45 seconds: a number card, a version tab, a clipping with a real headline, a score card, a
stamp, a reveal. A viewer who blinks must still find the board has moved.

## 3 · Hooks — the two laws

1. **"No way I don't know this."** The fan's pride: *You think you know Italia 90? Then you know about the water bottle.*
2. **"Come on — is this real?!"** The impossible, said straight: *A World Cup match. A goal. And a man walks down from the royal box and has it taken back.*

Line one is a concrete thing (a phone, a bottle, a whistle) in a concrete place and time. Never "Did you know", never
"In this video", never hype words (incredible, insane, amazing), no exclamation marks in the narration. The open loop the
first line creates is closed in the last.

The title is "The [object] That [consequence]" (the release check enforces it), with two alternates that use the other
hook law. Thumbnails: 2–3 words, one picture. Three Shorts, each ≤ 59 s, each opening on its own hook law.

## 4 · Atmosphere — the kit

Every FILE gets a **dossier** above its script (§7) with the world as it was. Use only what a source supports, or what
is plainly true of the era (no invented weather, no invented crowd chants):

- **The ground** — its name, its nickname, its capacity that day, the stand where it happened.
- **The time** — the season, the light (an afternoon kick-off in Valladolid in June, a night in Turin), the weather when
  a match report gives it.
- **The football** — the rules then (two points for a win, no golden goal yet, back-passes to the keeper), the kits,
  the ball, the money (a transfer fee, a wage, a bonus).
- **The media** — how people found out: teletext, the evening paper, a radio commentary, a fax. What the headline said
  the next morning (a real headline, quoted ≤ 15 words, from a listed source — it lands on the board as a clipping).
- **The people** — one human detail per chapter (a habit, a nickname, an age, what they said afterwards).

## 5 · The rules of the file

- **Every claim has a source** — numbered, listed under the script, cited in the beat. A source's title is written
  `Publisher — "Headline", date` so the board can lay its real headline on a clipping.
- **Disputes stay disputes.** VERSION A · VERSION B, each with who said it. Never pick one silently.
- **No invented dialogue.** A quote is verbatim from a listed source and short; otherwise it is paraphrased and the
  screen says *paraphrase*.
- **No invented minutes, scores, fees or crowds.** If no source gives the minute, the film does not say one.
- **Real people are drawn only from archive photographs the owner supplies**; every other figure gets an invented face.
- **Respect.** A death, an injury, a crime is told with its weight. LAUGH never laughs at a victim.

## 6 · Writing for the narrator (Kokoro George, Take B)

- Sentences of 6–18 words. A punch line of 2–4 words lands after a longer sentence: *He had spent his life catching things. … This one got away.*
- Years said as words in the narration (*nineteen ninety-six*); scores as *two-nil*, *three-all*.
- A name ending a sentence after a comma is landed on its own beat: *and the man who scored, Gili Landau.*
- "…" and " — " are held breaths. Use them where the viewer should lean in.
- Every new name goes into the pronunciation check (it is researched automatically; the owner's ear decides).

## 7 · The script document (`docs/fanlife/classified/fileNN-<slug>.script.md`)

1. **Header** — tab, narrator, length, panel count, draft and date.
2. **Dossier** — the story in five sentences; the world (§4); the cast (who, with the source); the timeline; what is
   disputed; what was left out and why.
3. **Script** — the FILE 01 table: `| Time | Panel · camera | Narration | On screen |`, chapter rows in bold.
   - Panel: the role (`P03`, `CH1`, the kit's `S02` dossier or `S03` tone background) and the camera move after `·`
     (push-in, pull-out, crane down/up, orbit, tilt, pan left/right, follow, dolly, hold).
   - On screen: `Caption: *…*` (the typed slug), `Tab: *VERSION A · …*`, `Score card *2–1*`, `**SMASH!**` (the shout,
     on the line's last word), `**CLASSIFIED**` (the stamp beat), `Sources` (the sources card).
   - Citations `[n]` at the end of the narration.
4. **Sources** — numbered, `Publisher — "Headline", date — what it supports`.
5. **Package** — three titles, the hook, three thumbnails (panel + 2–3 words), three Shorts (beats from–to + hook).
6. **Panels** — every role with a ready prompt for Nano Banana in Google Flow (free; §8).

`python3 -m comic.from_script docs/fanlife/classified/fileNN-….script.md` turns the table into the episode
(`comic/episodes/fileNN-….json`); `produce.validate` must return nothing before the owner reads it.

## 8 · Panels — made in Google Flow (free) or drawn

12–18 panels a FILE. One panel can carry several beats with different camera moves; the board's cards (captions,
version tabs, clippings, score cards) carry beats with no new art. The house look for a prompt:

> *Editorial comic panel, 16:9, bold black ink line, flat muted colours with a restrained paper-grain texture, three
> clear depth layers (foreground, subject, background), cinematic light. [THE SCENE]. Invented faces only, no text,
> no letters, no numbers, no logos, no club crests, no watermark.*

Upload to Drive `CLASSIFIED/<FILE>` named by role (`P03_…png`); the panel map picks them up.

## 10 · The edit the script is written for (docs/fanlife/71-continuous-camera-edit.md)
Write every beat for ONE hero on screen: one idea per line, one picture or one word or one document. A phrase is either
on a real document or typed on screen — never both, and never also said word for word on a card. Name the moment a
visual should land on (the word the circle or the stamp hits). No decorative props in the panel prompts.
