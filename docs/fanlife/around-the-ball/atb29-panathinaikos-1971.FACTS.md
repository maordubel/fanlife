## Facts

### 1 · The road in (qualification, coach)

- Panathinaikos entered as Greek champions of 1969–70 [BTLM · TRI]
- Lakis (Vassilis) Petropoulos left the bench in 1970; Ferenc Puskás took over [BTLM · TRI]
- Puskás became coach in 1970 (exact day disputed — see conflicts) [WPFP · UEFA14 · BTLM · CNN]
- Before Athens, Puskás's coaching had been minor: spells in North America and in Spain [BTLM (Spain, USA, Canada) · TRI (US league, Alavés)]
- Puskás as a player: Real Madrid forward, three European Cups with Madrid [WPFP · UEFA14 (his three winners' medals, nine years at Madrid)]
- Puskás was the star of Hungary's great 1950s side [DNH ("Golden Team") · TRI]
- He told his players they had nothing to fear from the opposition [UEFA15 · CNN (worked on their psychology so they would not fear opponents)]
- Puskás gave the side belief (said as paraphrase; the exact quotes are single-publisher) [UEFA14 (Antoniadis: "He instilled hope in us") · CNN (Domazos, translated: "we had no complex against them")]
- The players were not full professionals; Antoniadis worked in insurance [UEFA14 · TRI]
- Greek clubs had little European pedigree before this run [UEFA14 · TRI · FOG (never past the second round before)]

### 2 · Round one — Jeunesse Esch (Luxembourg)

- 16 September 1970, away: Jeunesse Esch 1–2 Panathinaikos; Antoniadis and Eleftherakis scored [PAOEH · RSSSF · TRI · WPEC]
- Antoniadis scored inside the first five or six minutes in Luxembourg [PAOEH (6') · RSSSF (5') · BTLM (within five minutes)]
- 30 September 1970, Athens: Panathinaikos 5–0; Antoniadis four, Eleftherakis one [PAOEH · RSSSF · TRI · BTLM (four)]
- Through 7–1 on aggregate [WPEC · TRI · WPEU] (BTLM: 7–2 — see conflicts)

### 3 · Round two — Slovan Bratislava (Czechoslovakia)

- 21 October 1970, Athens: Panathinaikos 3–0; Domazos, Antoniadis, Deliyannis [PAOEH · RSSSF · TRI]
- 4 November 1970, Bratislava: Slovan 2–1 Panathinaikos; Antoniadis scored [PAOEH · RSSSF · TRI · BTLM]
- Slovan's late winner came from Čapkovič [PAOEH · TRI]
- Through 4–2 on aggregate [WPEC · WPF · TRI]

### 4 · Quarter-final — Everton

- Everton were English champions of 1969–70 [TW · TRI · CNN]
- First leg: Tuesday 9 March 1971, Goodison Park, 1–1 [PAOEH · RSSSF · TW · UEFA15 · BTLM · WPEV · UEFAH]
- Antoniadis scored late, about nine minutes from time, from a move with Grammos [UEFA15 (nine from time) · RSSSF (81') · BTLM (81') · TRI (Grammos return pass)] (minute disputed — see conflicts)
- David Johnson equalised for Everton in the last minute / stoppage time [PAOEH (90') · TW · TRI · BTLM · WPEV (90')]
- Second leg: 24 March 1971, Athens (Leoforos), 0–0 [PAOEH · RSSSF · TW · WPEC · UEFAH]
- Panathinaikos through on the away goal [WPEC · WPF · UEFA14 · TW · BTLM · CNN]
- The ground in Athens held about 25,000 [TW · AV]
- Referee in Athens: Robert Héliès (France) [WPEC · TW]

### 5 · Semi-final — Red Star Belgrade

- Red Star were Yugoslav champions of 1969–70 [WPRS · RSSSFY]
- Red Star's coach was Miljan Miljanić [AV · KUR]
- First leg: 14 April 1971, Belgrade (Marakana), Red Star 4–1 Panathinaikos [PAOEH · RSSSF · KUR · CZ · AV · UEFA71]
- Stevan Ostojić scored a hat-trick for Red Star [PAOEH · BTLM · TRI · CZ · KUR · SM]
- Slobodan Janković scored Red Star's other goal, just before half-time [PAOEH (39') · TRI (39') · CZ (39') · KUR (40') · SM (39')]
- Aristidis Kamaras scored Panathinaikos's away goal, about ten minutes into the second half [RSSSF (56') · PAOEH (56') · AV (56') · CZ (55') · KUR (55') · TRI]
- Crowd in Belgrade: 93,500 [CZ · KUR] (AV: 90,000)
- Referee in Belgrade: Erich Linemayr (Austria) [TRI · KUR]
- Dragan Džajić was suspended for the return leg [TRI · NOV]
- Return leg about two weeks later: 28 April 1971, Athens (Leoforos), Panathinaikos 3–0 [WPEC · PAOEH · RSSSF · AV · BTLM · PP]
- Antoniadis scored in the opening minutes [PAOEH (1') · RSSSF (2') · BTLM (after two minutes) · TRI (first minute) · AV]
- Antoniadis's second came early in the second half [PAOEH (54') · RSSSF (55') · AV (55') · TRI (54') · BTLM]
- Kamaras made it 3–0 [PAOEH · RSSSF · AV · BTLM · TRI · CNN · UEFA71]
- Two goals for Antoniadis and one for Kamaras [UEFA71 · CNN · RSSSF · PAOEH]
- 4–4 on aggregate; Panathinaikos through on away goals [WPEC · WPF · UEFA14 · BTLM · TRI]
- Referee in Athens: José María Ortiz de Mendíbil (Spain) [WPEC · AV]

### 6 · Antoniadis

- Top scorer of the 1970–71 European Cup with 10 goals [WPEC · WPAA · UEFA14 · UEFA15 · BTLM · TRI · CNN · FOG]
- Panathinaikos scored 16 in the four rounds before the final; Antoniadis 10 of them [CNN · arithmetic from PAOEH/RSSSF scores]
- The Goodison goal is remembered as the "five nines" goal [UEFA15 · BTLM]
- Signed from Aspida Xanthi in 1968 [UEFA15 · BTLM]
- Greek league top scorer five times between 1970 and 1975 [UEFA15 · BTLM · WPAA · TRI]
- 39 league goals in 1971–72, one fewer than Gerd Müller in Europe that season [UEFA15 · BTLM · TRI]
- 187 league goals for Panathinaikos; 6 goals in 21 games for Greece [UEFA15 · BTLM · WPAA]

### 7 · Domazos and the side

- Mimis Domazos was captain [RSSSF · BTLM · TRI · FOG]
- His nickname was "The General" [UEFA15 · WPMD · FOG · PAO55]
- He joined the club as a teenager in 1959 [WPMD · TRI]
- Goalkeeper Takis Ikonomopoulos, sweeper Anthimos Kapsis, midfielder Kostas Eleftherakis among the key men [BTLM · TRI · PAO55]
- XI at Wembley: Ikonomopoulos; Tomaras, Sourpis, Kamaras, Vlachos, Eleftherakis, Domazos, Kapsis, Grammos, Antoniadis, Filakouris [RSSSF · CNN · FB · TRI]

### 8 · The final — Wembley, 2 June 1971

- Wednesday 2 June 1971, Wembley Stadium, London: Ajax 2–0 Panathinaikos [WPF · UEFA71 · RSSSF · PAO55 · FB · FOG · BTLM]
- Panathinaikos were the first Greek club in a European Cup final [WPEC · UEFA71 · PAO55]
- Ajax were coached by Rinus Michels, with Johan Cruyff [FOG · CNN · FB]
- Ajax had lost the 1969 final [WPF · TRI]
- Dick van Dijk headed Ajax ahead after five minutes [WPF · UEFA71 · RSSSF · FB · FOG · BTLM] (TRI: 4')
- The cross came from Piet Keizer [TRI · FOG]
- Arie Haan, a substitute, made it 2–0 late on; his shot deflected off Kapsis [WPF · UEFA71 · RSSSF · FB · FOG · TRI · BTLM] (minute disputed; PAO and CNN credit it as a Kapsis own goal — see conflicts)
- Attendance about 83,000 [WPF · RSSSF (83,179) · CNN (83,176) · BTLM · FB]
- Referee: Jack Taylor (England) [WPF · RSSSF · UEFA12 · FB]
- Ajax's first European Cup [BTLM · FOG]
- Thousands of Greek supporters travelled to London [GR · FB]

### 9 · Aftermath — the Intercontinental Cup

- Ajax declined to play; Panathinaikos represented Europe against Nacional of Montevideo [WPIC · PAOIC · PAOEH · TRI · FOG]
- 15 December 1971, Karaiskakis Stadium, Piraeus: Panathinaikos 1–1 Nacional [WPIC · PAOEH · TRI]
- Filakouris scored just after half-time; Luis Artime equalised about two minutes later [WPIC (48', 50') · PAOEH (48', 51') · TRI]
- Referee in Piraeus: (José) Favilli Neto (Brazil) [WPIC · TRI]
- 28 December 1971, Estadio Centenario, Montevideo: Nacional 2–1 Panathinaikos; Artime twice [WPIC · PAOEH · TRI]
- Filakouris scored Panathinaikos's goal at the very end in Montevideo [PAOEH (89') · TRI (final minute)]
- Nacional won 3–2 on aggregate [WPIC · TRI (from scores)]
- 1971 European Super Cup: no source read ties Panathinaikos to it — omit

### 10 · Still the only one

- The only Greek club to reach a European Cup / Champions League final [WPF · PAO55 (2026, "the only one to date") · UEFA15 (2015, only Greek side in a UEFA club final) · DNH (2014)]
- 1985: Panathinaikos reached the European Cup semi-final and lost to Liverpool [PAOEH · GR · WPEU · TRI]
- 1985 semi-final scores: Liverpool 4–0 at Anfield, 0–1 in Athens [PAOEH · WPEU]
- Puskás died in 2006 [WPFP (17.11.2006, Budapest) · UEFA14]
- In 2014 Athens honoured the 1971 side with an exhibition and a plaque, attended by Hungary's prime minister [UEFA14 · DNH]

### 11 · Greece under the colonels (only what ties to the club)

- Greece was under a military dictatorship (1967–74) during the run [AV · TRI · FB · BTLM]
- The regime used the club's success and backed it with money and bonuses [BTLM · AV · TRI]
- Claims that Red Star were bribed before the Athens leg circulated later, attributed to the dictator's widow; they were never substantiated [BTLM · TRI · AV]
- Red Star players have said for decades they believe the Athens match was not clean, without proof [NOV · TRI (denied by Yugoslav sources)] — film may say only "Red Star players still have doubts; nothing was ever proven"
