# Every Football Stories film is an experience: a judged hook, an edit with no slideshow, the hand-made graphics by data, and a gate that measures it (owner, 12.11.2026)

Owner: *"Every film must be an experience — viral, interesting, fun to watch, with a HOOK. Upgrade the brains: screenwriter, director, editor, producer, graphic designer. It is important to make a product at the highest level — in the editing, the script, the directing, the logic."* Earlier he rejected a film that looked like a slideshow and approved the hand-written DOCU storyboards (`studio/docu/films/*.py`) as "the graphic minimum from now on — and I want even more". What that became, one owner per decision (docs/fanlife/72):

**Screenwriter and director — the hook judge** (`studio/hook_judge.py`). The writer still returns three openings; the first is no longer spoken by default. Each is scored out of 100 against the HOOK LAWS of `writer.py`: a concrete stake in sentence one, the strongest moment before the calendar, a question the film answers late, sentence one inside the first ten seconds at the narrator's pace (`FIRST_WORDS`), no banned opener, no final score given away. A model is asked once, only when the best two are within `TIE` points, through the writer's own client and key failover; no key or a busy tier leaves the scores to decide, and a film never fails for it. The winner is spoken only where that is safe (same evidence as the opening pictures, every writer check still green); the three scores and the reason are on the script (`hook_judge`) and on the job (`hook`) for the desk.
- A film made from a pitch (`pitchBrief`, `auto_pick`) has the three directors each write, as the owner decided on 4.11.2026, and the same judge chooses after `script.validate_script`; there is still no second click (8.11.2026). When the free daily quota ends, three scripts become two, then the pitching director's one, said in the log.

**Editor — DOCU has a cut** (`studio/docu/editor.py`, drawn by `build.Film`). It never moves a time of the plan.
- No still holds longer than `MAX_STILL` (4 s): a longer one is cut into looks — wide → detail → reverse, then a related picture — on the music's beat grid when the mix's track is at hand (`audio.beat_grid`), otherwise on a word start. A detail frames the busiest part of the picture. The film's first look ends by `OPENING` (2.6 s).
- A full-frame card leaves after `MAX_CARD` (6 s) and the beat's picture carries the rest; a slammed word after `WORD_HOLD`. A held card creeps (`CREEP`); nothing stands dead (docs/fanlife/71).
- Every beat arrives by a transition chosen from what it is — cut, whip, tear, dive, scale match, fade — never the same three times running. None goes to white, none shakes (rule 142).
- The docs/71 rules hold in DOCU as in VOX: no picture treatment and no camera move three times in a row (the third plain still is a print on the desk), a chapter ends on a turn.
- A storyboard written by hand (no `in` on its beats) is drawn exactly as before. `STORIES_DOCU_EDIT=0` shows the old cut for comparison.

**Graphic designer — the hand-made devices, on data only** (`studio/docu/designer.py`). The adapter emits the city split, the league table, the chalk board, the verdict, a stamp, the typed rule card, the press cutting and a cut-out through the same `build.Film` implementations the hand-written films use — each only when the beat's data earns it, otherwise the device it always drew:
- cities: both places from the place book (`dossiers/places.json`); a final with no ground on record claims no city. Table: the record's own rows, contiguous from first place, the club lit, no drop line. Chalk: only a goal that a goal-detail claim describes as a free kick or a corner (the ledger holds no line-ups, so the board never draws one). Verdict and stamp: only once the beat's narration has told the result. Press: only a sourced headline whose picture is the cutting. Cut-out: only the owner's own, asked for by key.
- `Designer.sourced()` is rules 2 and 11 as code: a device that would print a word, a number or a place outside the ledger's claims, the place book and the beat's checked words is dropped.

**The moving-art library plays in DOCU.** The plan's `lib:` scene is the picture (painted in the club's colour) and the shot's person and object are cut-out layers. It is synced every run; before this only the classic engine drew it.

**Producer — the experience gate** (`studio/qa.py`). On the finished film's frames, each threshold a named constant with its reason: a change within `FIRST_CHANGE_S`, the stake on screen within `STAKE_BY_S`, no shot past `MAX_SHOT_S`, `MOTION_FLOOR` per shot and no `DEAD_RUN_S` of dead frames, `UNIQUE_ASSET_MIN` and `MAX_ASSET_SHOTS`, no flash, no shake, `WORST_JERK_PX`. A failure blocks `ready` in plain English and names the plan shots; the pipeline re-plans those shots once (`experience-replan.json`) and renders again before the owner is asked. Enforced for the DOCU engine; measured and reported, not enforced, for VOX, vox-hf and classic. The owner's own opening is not judged.

**Calm, everywhere.** No shot gives the camera an impulse, `flash` as an entry is a hard cut in new and saved plans (`look.CALM_ENTRY`), no white sheet on a trailer line or a win, the HyperFrames board neither jolts nor flashes, the substitution board lights once (rule 142, finished).

Held by `test_hook_judge`, `test_docu_editor`, `test_docu_designer`, `test_experience_gate`, `test_calm_camera`. Measured on six beats of the 1999/00 pilot, before → after: 6.2 → 22.6 picture shots a minute, longest still 13.4 s → 3.5 s, first change 12.4 s → 2.2 s, dead frames 581 → 5; the gate fails the old cut on three checks and passes the new one.
