# Every Football Stories film is an experience: a judged hook, an edit with no slideshow, the hand-made graphics by data, and a gate that measures it (owner, 12.11.2026)

Owner: *"Every film must be an experience — viral, interesting, fun to watch, with a HOOK. Upgrade the brains: screenwriter, director, editor, producer, graphic designer. It is important to make a product at the highest level — in the editing, the script, the directing, the logic."* Earlier he rejected a film that looked like a slideshow and approved the hand-written DOCU storyboards (`studio/docu/films/*.py`) as "the graphic minimum from now on — and I want even more". What that became, one owner per decision (docs/fanlife/72):

**Screenwriter and director — the hook judge** (`studio/hook_judge.py`). The writer still returns three openings; the first is no longer spoken by default. Each is scored out of 100 against the HOOK LAWS of `writer.py`: a concrete stake in sentence one, the strongest moment before the calendar, a question the film answers late, sentence one inside the first ten seconds at the narrator's pace (`FIRST_WORDS`), no banned opener, no final score given away. A model is asked once, only when the best two are within `TIE` points, through the writer's own client and key failover; no key or a busy tier leaves the scores to decide, and a film never fails for it. The winner is spoken only where that is safe (same evidence as the opening pictures, every writer check still green); the three scores and the reason are on the script (`hook_judge`) and on the job (`hook`) for the desk.
- A film made from a pitch (`pitchBrief`, `auto_pick`) has the three directors each write, as the owner decided on 4.11.2026, and the same judge chooses after `script.validate_script`; there is still no second click (8.11.2026). When the free daily quota ends, three scripts become two, then the pitching director's one, said in the log.

**Editor — DOCU has a cut** (`studio/docu/editor.py`, drawn by `build.Film`). It never moves a time of the plan.
- No still holds longer than `MAX_STILL` (4 s): a longer one is cut into looks — wide → detail → reverse, then a related picture — on the music's beat grid when the mix's track is at hand (`audio.beat_grid`), otherwise on a word start. A detail frames the busiest part of the picture. The film's first look ends by `OPENING` (2.6 s).
- A full-frame card leaves after `MAX_CARD` (6 s) and the beat's picture carries the rest; a slammed word after `WORD_HOLD`. A held card creeps (`CREEP`); nothing stands dead (docs/fanlife/71).
- Every beat arrives by a transition chosen from what it is — cut, whip, tear, dive, scale match, fade — never the same three times running. None goes to white, none shakes (rule 142).
- The docs/71 rules hold in DOCU as in VOX: no picture treatment and no camera move three times in a row (the third plain still is a print on the desk), a chapter ends on a turn.
- A storyboard written by hand (no `in` on its beats) is drawn exactly as before. `STORIES_DOCU_EDIT=0` shows the old cut for comparison.

**Graphic designer — the hand-made devices, on data only** (`studio/docu/designer.py`). The adapter emits the city split, the league table, the chalk board, the verdict, a stamp, the typed rule card, the press cutting and a cut-out through the same `build.Film` implementations the hand-written films use — each only when the beat's data earns it, otherwise the device it always drew:
- cities: both places from the place book (`dossiers/places.json`); a final with no ground on record claims no city. Table: the record's own rows, contiguous from first place, the club lit, no drop line. Chalk: only a goal that a goal-detail claim describes as a free kick or a corner (the ledger holds no line-ups, so the board never draws one). Verdict and stamp: only once the beat's narration has told the result. Press: only a sourced headline whose picture is the cutting. Cut-out: only the owner's own, asked for by key.
- `Designer.sourced()` is rules 2 and 11 as code: a device that would print a word, a number or a place outside the ledger's claims, the place book and the beat's checked words is dropped.

**The moving-art library plays in DOCU.** The plan's `lib:` scene is the picture (painted in the club's colour) and the shot's person and object are cut-out layers. It is synced every run; before this only the classic engine drew it.

**Producer — the experience gate** (`studio/qa.py`). On the finished film's frames, each threshold a named constant with its reason: a change within `FIRST_CHANGE_S`, the stake on screen within `STAKE_BY_S`, no shot past `MAX_SHOT_S`, `MOTION_FLOOR` per shot and no `DEAD_RUN_S` of dead frames, `UNIQUE_ASSET_MIN` and `MAX_ASSET_SHOTS`, no flash, no shake, `WORST_JERK_PX`. A failure blocks `ready` in plain English and names the plan shots; the pipeline re-plans those shots once (`experience-replan.json`) and renders again before the owner is asked. Enforced for the DOCU engine; measured and reported, not enforced, for VOX, vox-hf and classic. The owner's own opening is not judged.

**Calm, everywhere.** No shot gives the camera an impulse, `flash` as an entry is a hard cut in new and saved plans (`look.CALM_ENTRY`), no white sheet on a trailer line or a win, the HyperFrames board neither jolts nor flashes, the substitution board lights once (rule 142, finished).

Held by `test_hook_judge`, `test_docu_editor`, `test_docu_designer`, `test_experience_gate`, `test_calm_camera`. Measured on six beats of the 1999/00 pilot, before → after: 6.2 → 22.6 picture shots a minute, longest still 13.4 s → 3.5 s, first change 12.4 s → 2.2 s, dead frames 581 → 5; the gate fails the old cut on three checks and passes the new one.

## Batch 4 — the media index, the camera, three languages, the score sheet (owner, 12.11.2026)

Owner, after seeing batch 3's plan: *"More VOX editing. More HyperFrames editing. More documentary editing. More animation, more motion. As little static camera as possible — interesting camera movement. Smart use of the media libraries we have in GitHub and in Drive: real index management, correct use of pictures from the libraries. Directing that is interesting, viral, sweeping, dramatic. Make mini-dramas. Create tension. Create joy and happiness. Excitement."* Rule 142 stands through all of it: **movement means deliberate camera and layer motion, never jitter** — no shake, no flicker, no flash.

**Librarian — the media index** (`studio/media_index.py`). One record per picture and clip the studio can reach: the kit (beats, clips, cut-outs, art, props, the cached Wikimedia sets), the Drive inventory, the moving-art library and the Flow spec — id, source, path, kind, clubs, decade, subject tags (`SUBJECTS`), mood tags (`MOODS`: tension, joy, grief, defiance, calm), colour families, dominant colours, size, orientation, rights (owner-made · library · needs-check). Tags come only from names, the kit's own index entries, the inventory's paths and PIL; no model is asked and unknown stays unknown (rules 2, 11).
- `Picker.pick(beat)` is the one way a picture is chosen for a beat that has none: club, decade, subject, mood, fit for the frame and **freshness** (marked down when it is already in this film or in one of the last `RECENT_FILMS` films; never on more than `MAX_SHOTS` shots of a film). Another club's own art is always refused. A colour family is refused when the caller bans it — DOCU passes `Palette.render_bans`, so the recorded decision *"Rival colours may appear in photographs, opponents and neutral scenery"* (`common.py`) still decides how strict a film is.
- The generated file (`media-index.json`) is reused until a source changes and rides in the moving-art library's cache; `Ledger` (`STORIES_MEDIA_LEDGER`, restored and saved around every worker run) keeps which asset was on which shot of which film. `python -m studio.media_index report` is the desk's coverage report: per club by subject and mood, the most used pictures, and the gaps — the list of art worth making.
- DOCU's spare and related pictures come from the picker (batch 1's `SPARE` tuple is gone); the older engines' `look.pick` takes the least recently shown when a ledger is kept. A Drive file is a candidate only once it is in the kit (Actions → *Drive art into the studio*): the inventory lists, it does not download.

**Camera** (`studio/camera.py`, one grammar for DOCU and vox-hf; `pose()`/`frame_pil` give the PIL engines the same numbers). Eight named moves, each with a purpose: `dolly` (the walls close in), `crane` (the reveal), `track` (along the people), `rack` (one subject to the other), `orbit` (planes passing), `snap` (the hit, settled once — ease-out, nothing comes back), `pull` (stepping back), `breath` (a held frame that still moves). A still is two depth planes (the picture and its foreground band, read off the picture by `horizon`) and a vignette, so near things travel further than far ones; a cut-out is the nearest plane.
- The director chooses by the beat's emotion, then by what the picture shows (`choose`); `breath` under a card; never the same move three times running (docs/71).
- Calm by construction: every curve is eased and never stops (`glide`, `settle`), bounded by `SPEED_MAX`/`JERK_MAX` (the snap by `SNAP_*`); `flow()` is VOX's `dead_frames`/`worst_jerk` for the tween curves and `test_camera` holds every move at every length and intensity.

**Three languages in one film** (`studio/docu/explainer.py`). The plan decides per beat: **documentary** (art and the camera), **explainer** (VOX's pen, highlighter and odometer on the DOCU page: `timeline`, `number`, `bars`, beside the designer's table, city split and chalk board) or **kinetic** (type that arrives with the voice: a quote, a beat's one short line). Targets, named and reported by the gate: plain art at most `PLAIN_MAX` (55%) of the running time; at least `EXPLAINERS_PER_CHAPTER` explainer per chapter whose data supports one. Every explainer passes `Designer.sourced()` — a name, a minute or a number outside the ledger and the beat's own checked words is not printed.

**Director — the score sheet** (`studio/drama.py`). Per beat an emotion (tension, dread, release, joy, pride, grief, defiance, wonder, humour) and an intensity 0–1, derived without a model from what the beat is, the markers in its line and its place in the chapter and the film; plus the arc. The crafts follow it: the camera's move and travel; the pace (`editor.pace`: short looks as tension rises, ONE held look before the turn, a burst at the release — never under `MIN_SUB`); the transition; the music (`attach`: a strong beat leads the section's mood, `HUSH_DEPTH` before the turn, `LIFT_GAIN` on the release); the narrator's delivery; the colour (`build.GRADES`: cold for dread, grey for grief, the club's colour blooming over `BLOOM_S` on joy — a grade, never a flash); the match clock at a sourced minute and a goal's scoreline that changes on the cut.
- The writer writes every chapter as a **mini-drama** — a want, an obstacle, a clock, a turn, a payoff, a cliff-edge last line — and the film plants a question in its first fifteen seconds, withholds the outcome and pays it off after the midpoint, with one moment of tension and one of joy (`"drama": 1`). `script.mini_drama` says what is missing in plain English; the film's own script gets ONE rewrite, kept only if it is valid and closer to the shape. An option among three is reported, not rewritten (the free daily quota). A film never fails for it.

**Producer — the gate, extended** (`studio/qa.py`): `static shots` (no `STATIC_RUN_S` without measurable camera or layer motion; the share of the film that moves), `language mix`, `the arc` (enforced for a script written to the shape, reported for an older one), `library hygiene` (pictures the last films also showed — reported, never blocking).

Held by `test_media_index`, `test_camera`, `test_docu_languages`, `test_drama`, `test_experience_gate` (Batch4).

Measured on the same six beats of the 1999/00 pilot, batch 3 → batch 4, both by this gate: language mix documentary 82% / explainer 9% / kinetic 10% → 53% / 30% / 17%; named camera moves 0 (one-layer tweens) → 6 on two depth planes and a vignette; visible changes a minute 24.7 → 27.8; the gate passes both. Static seconds were already 0.0 and motion 100% in batch 3 — the camera changed what the motion IS, not how much of the film moves. The media index holds 989 records (375 on the build machine); the passage's arc check reports honestly that a 58-second excerpt never answers its opening question.
